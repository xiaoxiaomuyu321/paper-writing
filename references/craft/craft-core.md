# Craft core — the books' writing technique, condensed

This file is the standing authority on **how to write** for every journal in this skill. The two
books (Gastel & Day 8e, 2016 — journal papers; Turabian 7e, 2003 — research writing, Chicago
citation, theses) supply the complete technique set; the Nature-side fragments govern only
venue constraints (limits, formats, submission packages). When they conflict: Nature-family
journal → the venue convention wins; generic journal → the book wins.

Anchors: HTW = Gastel & Day 8e · Tur = Turabian 7e. Deep technique with worked examples:
`section-craft.md` (sections), `language-craft.md` (sentences/style/revision),
`argument-craft.md` (research argument), `citation-prose.md` (sources in prose). Original wording:
`python scripts/paper_kb.py query "<keywords>" --source "<book>"`.

## Sentence level

1. **One idea per sentence.** Simple declarative (subject–verb–object) as the workhorse; split
   sentences carrying multiple claims. *(HTW)*
2. **Front-load.** Main point first, details after; the first seven or eight words set the
   sentence — no long introductory phrases, short concrete subjects, subject and verb together.
   *(both)*
3. **Old → new.** Given information at sentence start, new information at the end; this — not
   habit — decides active vs passive. Active is the default (more precise, less wordy); passive
   only where active would break old-new order or in Methods. Name the agent, including "we".
   *(both)*
4. **Verbs carry the action.** Turn abstract nouns into verbs ("Examination… was carried out" →
   "I examined"); no empty verb (have/do/make/be) introducing a buried action.
   *(both)*
5. **Concision is a system.** Write for the hundreds of slightly-related readers, not the
   half-dozen of your exact niche; the longer word is jargon unless it says more; "a
   considerable amount of" → much; big things have little names. Ban vague value words
   (important/interesting/significant) in claims — the claim sentence must carry the specificity.
   *(both)*
6. **Plain international English.** One-meaning words, no idioms or allusions; keep optional
   clarifying words that prevent misreading. The goal is to communicate the science, not to
   impress with English. *(HTW)*
7. **Tense is epistemic.** Present = established/published knowledge; past = your own work.
   Attribution verbs for a source's text stay present ("argues", not "argued").
   *(HTW + Tur)*

## Paragraph and section level

8. **Topic sentence first** (main point, then support); one function per paragraph; one idea
   leads smoothly to the next; add a transition only where a real relation exists.
   *(both)*
9. **Front-load sections too.** Open Results with the main result; open Discussion with the
   direct answer to the research question; the introduction funnels broad → specific and ends
   with the objective/hypothesis. *(HTW)*
10. **Claim discipline.** Shine a spotlight on one area of the truth — scope every claim to the
    data; calibrate the verb to the evidence; state strengths and limitations yourself; never
    fudge data that don't fit. In arguments, supply both the warrant (why the reason is
    relevant) and the evidence. *(both)*
11. **Introduce every borrowed element** — source, quotation, figure, table — with a sentence
    that states the claim it supports. *(Tur)*

## Revision level

12. **"Good writing tends to be largely a matter of good revising"** — expect many rounds;
    self-revise, then get outside eyes, then revise again. *(HTW)*
13. **Revise top-down:** outer frame (introduction/conclusion) → organization → sections →
    paragraphs → sentences → finally style. *(both)*
14. **Run the 8 Cs pass:** compliance, completeness, composition, correctness, clarity,
    consistency, conciseness, courtesy — each with its own check items (see
    `language-craft.md` §7). *(HTW)*
15. **Gain distance:** set the draft aside; print it or change typeface/margins; read it aloud;
    proofread numbers one by one and, for the last pass, word-by-word or backward. Never
    outsource proofreading wholesale. *(both)*
16. **Plagiarism is perceived, not intended:** apply the author-recognition test ("would the
    source's author recognize any of it as hers?") and the look-away paraphrase procedure; exact
    words always get quotation marks *and* a citation. *(Tur + HTW)*
