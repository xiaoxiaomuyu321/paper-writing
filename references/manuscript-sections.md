# Manuscript section contracts

Use these as semantic contracts, not fixed word-count formulas. The target journal and reporting guideline override them. For *how* to write each section — the book's distilled techniques — see [craft/section-craft.md](craft/section-craft.md), which is the authority on writing technique for every journal.

## Title, keywords, and title page

The title states the topic and main relationship/outcome with searchable terms; include study design when required. Avoid unsupported priority claims, hype, unexplained abbreviations, formulas, and filler such as “A study of.” Keywords complement rather than merely repeat the title and should use controlled vocabulary when the field provides one.

The title page normally includes author names and affiliations, corresponding-author details, ORCID iDs where requested, running title, counts, funding, disclosures, and article-type-specific declarations.

## Abstract

Match the journal's structured or unstructured format. Cover context/gap, objective, essential design and setting, participants/materials, primary methods, main quantitative results with denominators/effect sizes/uncertainty when relevant, bounded conclusion, and registration/data identifiers if required. Write it last and audit every number against the body. Do not introduce unsupported claims, citations, or abbreviations the journal forbids.

## Introduction

Move from important known context to the unresolved gap, why it matters, and the precise objective/hypothesis. Use selective, verified citations. Do not turn it into a comprehensive review or reveal the study's detailed results.

## Methods

Enable evaluation and replication. Report design, setting and dates, sampling/eligibility, materials/instruments/software and versions, interventions/procedures, variables/outcomes, controls, randomization/blinding where relevant, sample-size rationale, preprocessing, exclusions, statistical/qualitative analysis, sensitivity analyses, ethics/consent, registration, and deviations from protocol. Distinguish prespecified from exploratory analyses. Link protocols, code, and data where appropriate.

## Results

Follow the Methods and the analysis plan in a logical sequence. Start with sample/data flow and baseline or descriptive information, then primary, secondary, sensitivity, subgroup, and adverse/unexpected findings as applicable. Report absolute values and denominators alongside derived measures. Give effect sizes and uncertainty, not P values alone. State observations without interpretation and avoid repeating complete tables in prose.

## Discussion and conclusion

Open with the direct answer to the research question. Interpret the major findings against the full relevant evidence; explain plausible mechanisms and alternatives; separate supported inference from speculation; discuss strengths, limitations, bias, uncertainty, generalizability, and implications. Do not use causal language for a design that supports association only. The conclusion must be no stronger or broader than the data.

## Figures, tables, equations, and supplements

Use figures for patterns/relationships and tables for exact values. Make legends self-contained: define groups, symbols, error bars, tests, sample sizes, normalization, exclusions, and reuse permissions. Use accessible colors and readable labels at final size. Keep units and significant digits coherent. Number equations and define symbols on first use. Supplements must be versioned, cited in the main text, and reviewed with the manuscript; do not hide essential methods or outcome switching there.

## References and declarations

Cite primary/original evidence when possible. Verify authors, title, venue, year, volume/pages/article number, DOI or other persistent identifier, and correction/retraction status. Label preprints and datasets accurately. Do not cite a source merely because it discusses the topic; it must support the adjacent claim.

Add acknowledgments, author contributions, funding/grant numbers, conflicts, ethics/consent, registration, data availability, code availability, materials availability, and AI-use disclosure as required. Ensure contributor roles and author order have explicit human agreement.
