# Theses and dissertations

Use this route for theses, dissertations, and long student research reports — not for journal articles (those follow [workflow.md](workflow.md) and the journal's instructions).

## Governing requirements, in order

1. The university/department's current formatting, front-matter, and submission regulations (templates, fonts, margins, binding, repository/deposit requirements, approval forms).
2. The advisor's expectations (structure, length, citation system, chapter layout).
3. Discipline conventions and the applicable citation style (see [chicago-citation.md](chicago-citation.md) when Chicago is required).
4. The bundled books for craft: Book 2 (Turabian) covers thesis/dissertation format, submission, and the full research process; Book 1 (Gastel & Day) covers the journal-paper skills the work will be built on.

Record consequential assumptions and use explicit placeholders such as `[VERIFY DEPARTMENT TEMPLATE]`; never invent committee rules, page limits, or submission requirements.

## Process contract

1. **Question and claim.** Move from topic to a research question, then to a working hypothesis, then to a defensible claim. State the project as: topic X, because I want to find out how/why Y, so that I can help others understand Z. Choose the question type deliberately (conceptual, practical, or applied).
2. **Sources.** Search systematically (library catalogs, periodical indexes, bibliographic trails, reference librarians), evaluate relevance and reliability separately, and look beyond the usual references. Read generously first, critically second; look for creative agreement and creative disagreement.
3. **Notes.** Take systematic notes with templates; summarize, paraphrase, or quote deliberately; write as you read; record full source data at note time. Guard against inadvertent plagiarism and against inappropriate assistance.
4. **Argument and plan.** Assemble claim, reasons, evidence, warrants, and responses to anticipated objections; convert the storyboard into an outline with key terms and subheads; order the reasons and make the order explicit with transitions.
5. **Draft and revise.** Draft in the way that works, develop productive habits, then revise: introduction/claim first, body coherence, paragraphs, a cool-down paraphrase pass, and sentence-level revision (first few words, diagnosis, word choice).
6. **Tables and figures.** Choose verbal vs. visual by question; design tables and figures for clarity and communicate data ethically (no manipulated baselines, consistent units, complete legends).
7. **Final introduction and conclusion; title last.**

Query Book 2 for each step, e.g. `query "working hypothesis claim" --source "A Manual for Writers"`, `query "note templates summarize paraphrase" --source "A Manual for Writers"`, `query "storyboard outline" --source "A Manual for Writers"`.

## Structure contract

Follow the institutional template; the canonical order it serves is:

- **Front matter:** title page (per template), abstract (single paragraph or structured, per template), table of contents with page numbers, lists of figures/tables/abbreviations, acknowledgments, (optional) dedication.
- **Body:** chapters following the discipline's logic — commonly Introduction, Literature/Background, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion — or discipline-specific chaptering (case studies, experiments, theoretical chapters). Keep terminology consistent across chapters; every chapter should be readable standalone.
- **Back matter:** references/bibliography in the chosen citation system, appendices (each cited from the body), (optional) vita.

## Format and submission contract

- Apply the template mechanically: fonts, spacing, margins, heading levels, page numbering (front matter vs. body), line numbering if required, binding/copy counts.
- Verify the abstract and title against the final text; regenerate the table of contents and all page numbers after the final layout pass.
- Check every reference against the original record (the book gives citation *form*, not verification); confirm the citation system matches the department requirement.
- Prepare the submission package per the regulations: approval/signature forms, committee copies, electronic deposit (repository, format, license/embargo), copyright/permissions for substantial quoted material.
- Re-audit after any post-defense revision: local changes can create global inconsistencies (abstract, TOC, cross-references, chapter numbers).
