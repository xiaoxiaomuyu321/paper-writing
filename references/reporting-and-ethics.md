# Reporting, integrity, and reproducibility

## Choose the reporting guideline by study design

Search the current EQUATOR library and the target journal; use extensions when they fit the population, intervention, data type, or method.

| Study/report type | Common starting point |
|---|---|
| Randomized trial | CONSORT (current version) and relevant extension |
| Trial protocol | SPIRIT |
| Observational cohort/case-control/cross-sectional | STROBE |
| Systematic review/meta-analysis | PRISMA 2020 and relevant extension |
| Diagnostic accuracy | STARD |
| Prediction model | TRIPOD and current extension |
| Case report | CARE |
| Qualitative research | SRQR or COREQ |
| Animal in-vivo experiment | ARRIVE 2.0 |
| Quality improvement | SQUIRE |
| Economic evaluation | CHEERS |
| Clinical practice guideline | AGREE/RIGHT as applicable |

A reporting checklist improves completeness; it does not certify study quality. Populate manuscript page/line locations only after pagination stabilizes.

## Authorship and contributorship

Apply the target journal's criteria. In biomedical contexts, verify all ICMJE authorship criteria; use CRediT's 14-role taxonomy to describe contributions but do not treat a role assignment as automatic authorship. Obtain explicit agreement on author list/order, corresponding-author responsibilities, contribution statements, and accountability before submission. Acknowledge non-author help with consent where required.

## Ethics, registration, conflicts, and overlapping work

Verify human/animal approvals, consent, protocol/registration identifiers, dates, and any exemptions. Explain protocol deviations and outcome changes. Disclose financial and non-financial relationships, funding, sponsor roles, related manuscripts, preprints, prior conference versions, reused material, and permissions. Do not submit the same manuscript to multiple journals simultaneously. Use COPE guidance when authorship, plagiarism/text recycling, data, peer review, corrections, or misconduct issues arise.

## AI-assisted work

Check the current journal/publisher policy. AI tools are not authors. Humans remain responsible for accuracy, originality, confidentiality, citations, images, code, and disclosures. Record tool, version, purpose, and material human verification when disclosure is required. If AI was part of the research method, report enough detail for evaluation and replication. Do not upload confidential manuscripts, unpublished data, or reviewer material to an external AI service without authorization and compatible terms.

## Open and reproducible research

For data, code, materials, protocols, and analysis plans, follow consent, privacy, security, intellectual-property, funder, repository, and journal constraints. Prefer a trusted repository and persistent identifier. State what is available, where, under what access/licence conditions, and why any restriction exists. Cite datasets/software as research outputs. Keep a README, environment/software versions, data dictionary, licenses, and analysis entry point.

The TOP framework is a useful general audit for registration, protocol, analysis plan, materials, code, data, and reporting transparency. For computer science/engineering, also check the venue's artifact-evaluation or reproducibility rules.

## Statistical and citation integrity

Report estimands/outcomes, analysis population, missing-data handling, assumptions, multiplicity, effect sizes, uncertainty, software/version, and prespecified versus exploratory analyses. Preserve negative and null findings. Do not infer equivalence from non-significance.

For each citation, verify the original source and claim support. Check Crossref/DOI metadata or an authoritative disciplinary index, and inspect correction/retraction status. Never manufacture a DOI, PMID, page range, quotation, or bibliographic field.
