# End-to-end workflow

## 1. Create the paper brief

Capture: paper type; study design; central question; one-sentence answer; novelty relative to named prior work; audience; target journal; word/figure/reference limits; reporting guideline; data/code availability; ethics/registration identifiers; author roles; and missing inputs. Convert unresolved items into an assumption and verification register.

For translation requests, first reconstruct the scientific meaning and terminology. Do not translate ambiguous Chinese literally. Mark ambiguities and preserve numbers, symbols, identifiers, citations, and scope.

## 2. Build the argument before prose

Create a claim-evidence table:

| ID | Claim | Direct support | Limits/alternatives | Planned location | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Specific, bounded claim | Result, figure, dataset, or verified citation | Boundary conditions | Results/Discussion | verified/open |

Then create a story map: problem → gap → objective/hypothesis → approach → decisive results → interpretation → contribution/limits. Remove results that do not advance the central argument or place them in supplements.

Draft figures and tables early. Each should answer one question, be interpretable with its legend, use consistent labels/units, expose uncertainty and sample size, and map to a Methods description and a Results statement.

## 3. Draft in an evidence-friendly order

Recommended default order: Methods → Results → figures/tables/legends → Discussion → Introduction → Abstract → Title. Change the order when the user's materials or genre make another sequence more efficient. Use the section contracts in `manuscript-sections.md` for *what each section must contain* and the book's distilled techniques in `craft/section-craft.md` for *how to write it* (the books are the authority on writing technique).

Write paragraph-level message sentences first. One paragraph should normally do one rhetorical job; the first sentence states the job, the middle supplies evidence/reasoning, and the final sentence closes or transitions. Place citations next to the exact claims they support.

## 4. Revision passes

Run separate passes so fluency does not hide scientific defects:

1. **Scientific validity:** claims match design, data, analysis, and uncertainty; causal language is justified.
2. **Argument:** the gap, objective, result sequence, interpretation, and contribution form one line of reasoning.
3. **Reporting completeness:** apply the relevant checklist and journal requirements item by item.
4. **Cross-document consistency:** numbers, group names, units, denominators, statistical tests, figure/table references, registration IDs, author details, and disclosure statements agree everywhere.
5. **Citation integrity:** verify metadata, claim support, original source preference, correction/retraction status, and journal style.
6. **Scientific English:** improve clarity, cohesion, concision, terminology, tense, voice, and hedging without changing meaning — run the books' revision system from `craft/language-craft.md` (top-down order, 11-question audit, 8 Cs pass, distance and proofreading mechanics).
7. **Format and submission:** file types, anonymization, word counts, line numbering, resolution, supplementary naming, and required forms.

## 5. Final verification record

Produce a compact audit with `pass`, `needs author confirmation`, or `not applicable` for:

- target-journal instructions accessed and dated;
- reporting checklist selected and completed;
- all tables/figures cited and non-duplicative;
- abstract/main text/data mutually consistent;
- statistics and units checked;
- references verified and retractions/corrections checked;
- authorship, funding, conflicts, ethics, registration, data/code, and AI disclosures present;
- submission files open correctly; and
- unresolved placeholders listed.

Do not describe a manuscript as submission-ready while any material item remains unknown.
